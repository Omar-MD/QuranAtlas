# QuranAtlas Product Vision

## Product Thesis

QuranAtlas is a reader-first Qur'an application that helps Muslims move from reading the Qur'an to living with the Qur'an.

The product must preserve the simplicity, dignity, and reliability of a primary Qur'an reader while quietly adding the context, structure, and guidance needed for meaningful tadabbur.

The app is not trying to replace scholars, tafsir, teachers, or personal effort. It is trying to remove the common barriers that prevent everyday Muslims from reflecting deeply:

- not knowing where to begin
- lacking context
- feeling disconnected from the verse
- not understanding key themes
- reading too quickly
- being overwhelmed by tafsir
- forgetting insights
- not converting reflection into action

## Product Image

QuranAtlas should feel like:

> A clean Qur'an mushaf-reader with a wise guide beside it.

The default experience is peaceful reading.  
The deeper experience is guided discovery.

The user should not feel they are using an academic research tool by default. They should feel:

> “I am reading the Qur'an, and when I pause, the app helps me notice what I would otherwise miss.”

## Core Promise

QuranAtlas helps users:

1. read the Qur'an beautifully and reliably
2. understand the immediate meaning of each verse
3. see the verse inside its passage and themes
4. reflect through guided prompts
5. connect the verse to their heart, life, and actions
6. revisit insights over time

## Non-Negotiables

### 1. Reader-first

The reader is sacred. It must remain fast, calm, and trustworthy.

Reflection features must not clutter the reading experience.

### 2. Scholarly-grounded

The app must distinguish between:

- Qur'anic text
- translation
- tafsir
- scholarly claim
- curated reflection
- personal user reflection
- AI-assisted explanation

These categories must never be blurred.

### 3. Progressive depth

The app should not dump knowledge.

It should reveal deeper layers only when the user asks or when the UX context naturally supports it.

### 4. Guided, not passive

The app should not simply give answers.

It should ask excellent questions that help the user think, feel, connect, and act.

### 5. Personal, not speculative

The app should help users relate verses to their lives, but never invent religious meanings or over-personalize the Qur'an in a way that detaches from tafsir and context.

## Product North Star

The user opens the app to read Qur'an.

They pause on an ayah.

The app helps them understand the ayah, see its place, reflect on it, and leave with one meaningful insight or action.

That is the product.
